# 📖 MODULE 1: UK FINANCIAL REGULATION (UKFR)

## ─── UNIT 1: Financial Services Environment & Products ───

### 1.1 Role of Money & the Bank of England

**Functions of Money:**
- Medium of exchange
- Store of value
- Unit of account
- Standard of deferred payment

**Bank of England (BoE):**
- Sets the base rate (Monetary Policy Committee — MPC meets 8x per year)
- Lender of last resort
- Issues banknotes
- Manages UK gold and foreign currency reserves
- Regulates payment systems

**Key Distinction:**
> The BoE's Financial Policy Committee (FPC) = macro-prudential (system-wide stability)
> The MPC = monetary policy (inflation target: 2%)

---

### 1.2 Financial Institutions

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Banks** | Accept deposits, lend money, operate payment systems |
| **Building Societies** | Mutual lenders — owned by members; majority of assets must be residential mortgages |
| **Credit Unions** | Member-owned co-operatives; must share a "common bond"; max loan often £25,000 |
| **Insurance companies** | Life and general insurance |
| **Investment firms** | Fund management, stockbroking |

**Mutual vs Proprietary:**
- Mutual = owned by members (no shareholders) → Building societies, credit unions
- Proprietary = owned by shareholders → Banks, most insurers

---

### 1.3 UK Taxation — Key Numbers to Memorise

| Tax | Key Points |
|-----|-----------|
| **Income Tax** | Basic rate 20% / Higher rate 40% / Additional rate 45% |
| **Personal Allowance** | £12,570 (2025/26 — frozen until at least 2028) |
| **Capital Gains Tax** | Annual exempt amount: £3,000 (2025/26). Rate: 18% basic / 24% higher (residential property) |
| **Inheritance Tax** | 0% on estates under £325,000 (nil-rate band); 40% above. Residence nil-rate band: £175,000 (if main home passed to direct descendants) |
| **Stamp Duty (SDLT)** | 0% up to £125,000; 2% £125k–£250k; 5% £250k–£925k; 10% £925k–£1.5m; 12% above. **FTB relief**: 0% up to £300,000; 5% £300k–£500k (max purchase £500,000) |
| **ISA limit** | £20,000 per year (2025/26 — frozen until at least 2030) |
| **Pension Lifetime Allowance** | Abolished April 2024 |
| **Annual Pension Allowance** | £60,000 (2025/26) |

**State Benefits** (know these exist and their purpose — not amounts):
- Universal Credit, Housing Benefit, Council Tax Reduction
- State Pension, Pension Credit
- Child Benefit

### 1.4 Investment Products

**Direct Investments:**
| Product | Key Feature |
|---------|-------------|
| Cash / Deposits | Low risk, FSCS protected up to £120,000 (from Dec 2025) |
| Fixed-interest securities (Gilts/Bonds) | Government (gilts) or corporate bonds; income + capital |
| Shares / Equities | Part ownership of company; dividends + capital growth |
| Property | High entry cost, illiquid, but tangible asset |

**Collective Investments:**
| Product | Structure |
|---------|-----------|
| Unit Trusts | Open-ended; investors buy units; fund manager buys assets |
| OEICs (Open-Ended Investment Companies) | Similar to unit trusts but structured as company |
| Investment Trusts | Closed-ended; listed on stock exchange |
| Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) | Tracks index; traded on exchange |

**Tax Wrappers:**
- **ISA** — Stocks & Shares ISA, Cash ISA, Lifetime ISA (LISA), Innovative Finance ISA
- **Pension** — Contributions get tax relief; growth is tax-free; 25% tax-free lump sum on drawdown
- **VCT / EIS / SEIS** — Higher-risk tax incentives for investing in small companies

## ─── UNIT 2: UK Financial Services Regulation ───

### 2.1 Regulatory Framework — MUST KNOW

**The "Twin Peaks" Model (post-2012):**

```
HM Treasury (Government)
        │
   Bank of England
   ┌──────┴──────┐
  FCA           PRA
```

| Body | Full Name | Responsible For |
|------|-----------|-----------------|
| **FCA** | Financial Conduct Authority | Conduct regulation of ALL firms; consumer protection; market integrity |
| **PRA** | Prudential Regulation Authority | Safety & soundness of banks, insurers, major investment firms |
| **FPC** | Financial Policy Committee (BoE) | Macro-prudential — system-wide financial stability |

**Key Legislation:**
- **Financial Services & Markets Act 2000 (FSMA 2000)** — Foundation of modern regulation; created FSA
- **Financial Services Act 2012** — Created FCA + PRA (abolished FSA)
- **Consumer Credit Act 1974 (updated 2006)** — Governs credit agreements
- **Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR** — Personal data rights
- **Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (MLR17)** — AML framework
- **Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (POCA)** — Criminalises money laundering

---

### 2.2 FCA — Objectives & Powers

**FCA's 3 Statutory Objectives:**
1. **Consumer Protection** — secure appropriate protection for consumers
2. **Market Integrity** — protect and enhance the integrity of UK financial markets
3. **Competition** — promote effective competition in the interests of consumers

**FCA Powers:**
- Grant/withdraw authorisation
- Investigate firms and individuals
- Issue fines
- Prosecute market abuse
- Ban financial promotions

**Consumer Duty (introduced July 2023):**
> Firms must act to deliver **good outcomes** for retail customers in four areas:
> 1. Products & Services
> 2. Price & Value
> 3. Consumer Understanding
> 4. Consumer Support

**Treating Customers Fairly (TCF) — 6 Outcomes:**
1. Consumers can be confident they are dealing with firms where TCF is central
2. Products/services marketed are designed for the target market
3. Consumers given clear information before, during, and after purchase
4. Advice is suitable for individual circumstances
5. Products perform as expected; consumers get what they were told
6. Consumers do not face unreasonable barriers to change product, switch, submit claims or complain

---

### 2.3 Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

**Three stages of Money Laundering:**
1. **Placement** — introducing criminal cash into the financial system
2. **Layering** — disguising the trail (e.g., multiple transfers)
3. **Integration** — funds re-enter the economy appearing legitimate

**AML Obligations on mortgage advisers:**
- Customer Due Diligence (CDD) — verify identity BEFORE the business relationship
- Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) — for higher-risk clients (PEPs, non-resident, unusual transactions)
- Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) — must file with National Crime Agency (NCA); **do NOT tip off the customer**
- Appoint a **MLRO** (Money Laundering Reporting Officer)

**Key Definitions:**
- **PEP** = Politically Exposed Person (senior public figure)
- **Tipping off** = criminal offence — telling a suspect a SAR has been filed

---

### 2.4 Data Protection (UK GDPR)

**7 Principles of UK GDPR:**
1. Lawfulness, fairness and transparency
2. Purpose limitation
3. Data minimisation
4. Accuracy
5. Storage limitation
6. Integrity and confidentiality (security)
7. Accountability

**Individual Rights:**
- Right of access (Subject Access Request — respond within 1 month)
- Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten")
- Right to rectification
- Right to portability
- Right to object to automated decision-making

---

### 2.5 Complaints, Compensation & Ombudsman

| Body | Role | Limit |
|------|------|-------|
| **FOS** (Financial Ombudsman Service) | Free dispute resolution for consumers vs firms | Awards up to **£445,000** (2025/26) |
| **FSCS** (Financial Services Compensation Scheme) | Pays out if authorised firm fails | Deposits: **£120,000** per person per institution (from Dec 2025). Temporary High Balance: **£1,400,000** for up to 6 months. Investments: **£85,000**. Insurance (compulsory): **100%, no limit**. Insurance (non-compulsory): **90%, no upper limit** |

**Complaint Handling Rules:**
- Acknowledge complaint promptly
- Investigate and issue Final Response within **8 weeks** for most complaints
- Give consumer the right to refer to FOS if not satisfied

---

### 2.6 "Fit and Proper" Requirements

Applies to employees in **controlled functions** who need FCA approval:
- Honesty, integrity, reputation
- Competence and capability
- Financial soundness

**Senior Managers & Certification Regime (SM&CR):**
- Senior Managers = FCA-approved; individual accountability
- Certified persons = firm must certify annually
- Conduct Rules apply to ALL staff

# 📖 MODULE 2: MORTGAGES

## ─── UNIT 3: Mortgage Law, Policy, Practice and Markets ───

### 3.1 Property Law Basics

**Ownership Types:**
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Freehold** | Owns land and building outright, indefinitely |
| **Leasehold** | Owns property for a set period (often 99–999 years); pays ground rent to freeholder |
| **Commonhold** | Collective ownership of flat blocks; each flat owner holds freehold of their own unit |

**Joint Ownership:**
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Joint tenancy** | Equal shares; right of survivorship (passes to co-owner on death) — cannot will your share |
| **Tenancy in common** | Defined shares; no right of survivorship — can leave share in will |

**Land Registration:**
- Land Registry holds title to all registered land in England & Wales
- Title deeds replaced by official registers
- **Title absolute** = best title (no disputes, clearly owned)

---

### 3.2 The Conveyancing Process

```
1. Offer accepted
2. Instructing solicitors / conveyancers
3. Searches (local authority, water, environmental, drainage)
4. Survey / valuation
5. Mortgage offer issued
6. Exchange of contracts → legally committed; buyer pays deposit (usually 10%)
7. Completion → keys handed over; funds transferred
8. Registration at Land Registry
```

**Key Searches:**
- **Local authority search** — planning, road proposals, conservation areas
- **Water/drainage search** — sewers, water mains
- **Environmental search** — flood risk, contamination

---

### 3.3 UK Mortgage Market

**Types of Borrowers:**
- First-time buyers (FTBs)
- Home movers
- Remortgagers
- Buy-to-let (BTL) landlords
- Right-to-buy (RTB) — purchase council property at a discount

**Special Schemes (know these):**
| Scheme | Purpose |
|--------|---------|
| **Shared Ownership** | Buy a share (25–75%) of a property; pay rent on rest; can "staircase" up to 100% |
| **Help to Buy: Equity Loan** | **CLOSED** to new applications (ended March 2023). Government provided 20% equity loan (40% London) for new builds |
| **Right to Buy** | Council tenants can buy their home at discount. **Major reforms from Nov 2024**: max discount reduced to £16,000–£38,000 by region; min tenancy now 10 years (was 3). New builds post-2025 exempt for 35 years |
| **Guarantor Mortgage** | Third party (often parent) guarantees the loan |
| **Mortgage Guarantee Scheme** | Government guarantees part of loan enabling 95% LTV mortgages. **Now permanent from July 2025** ("Freedom to Buy") |

**Equity Release:**
| Type | How it works |
|------|-------------|
| **Lifetime Mortgage** | Loan secured on home; interest rolls up; repaid when property sold (usually on death) |
| **Home Reversion Plan** | Sell part/all of home to provider; retain right to live there; provider gets share of sale proceeds |

## ─── UNIT 4: Mortgage Applications ───

### 4.1 The Mortgage Adviser's Role

**Regulated Mortgage Contract Criteria (all 3 must apply):**
1. Lender provides credit to an individual
2. Obligation secured on land in UK
3. At least 40% of land used (or intended to be used) as a dwelling

**Advice Process (COBS-like for mortgages):**
1. Initial disclosure / fact find
2. Assess needs + objectives
3. Research suitable products
4. Present recommendation + explain reasons
5. Client agrees → application submitted
6. Mortgage offer → client accepts → completion

---

### 4.2 Assessing Affordability

**Income Multiples:** Old approach — lenders used 3x–4.5x income. Now supplemented by full affordability assessment.

**FCA Affordability Rules (Mortgage Market Review 2014):**
- Assess income AND expenditure (debt commitments, essential outgoings)
- **Stress test** — can client afford repayments if interest rate rises? (Typically test at current rate + 3%)
- Self-employed: usually last 2–3 years' accounts or SA302s
- Interest-only: must have credible repayment vehicle

**Income Types:**
- Employed — basic salary, regular overtime, bonus (lenders vary on how much they count)
- Self-employed — net profit / salary + dividends from limited company
- Rental income — BTL lenders typically allow 75–85% of gross rental income

---

### 4.3 Property Valuation

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Basic Valuation** | Done for lender; confirms property worth lending against |
| **Homebuyer's Report (RICS Level 2)** | More detail; highlights defects; for standard properties |
| **Full Structural Survey (RICS Level 3)** | Most comprehensive; for older/unusual properties |

**Loan-to-Value (LTV) — CRITICAL FORMULA:**
```
LTV % = (Loan Amount ÷ Property Value) × 100

Example: £180,000 loan on £200,000 property
LTV = (180,000 ÷ 200,000) × 100 = 90%
```
- Higher LTV = higher risk = higher interest rate
- Most lenders cap at 95% LTV for residential

---

## ─── UNIT 5: Mortgage-Related Protection Products ───

### 5.1 Life Assurance for Mortgages

| Product | Best For | Type |
|---------|----------|------|
| **Decreasing term assurance** | Repayment mortgage — loan reduces so cover decreases | Term |
| **Level term assurance** | Interest-only mortgage — loan stays level | Term |
| **Whole of life** | Estate planning / IHT; guarantees payout | Permanent |
| **Critical Illness Cover (CIC)** | Pays lump sum on diagnosis of listed conditions | Can be standalone or added to life |
| **Income Protection (PHI)** | Replaces % of salary if unable to work | Long-term |

**MPPI (Mortgage Payment Protection Insurance):**
- Short-term insurance covering mortgage repayments
- Usually covers: accident, sickness, unemployment
- Typically pays for 12–24 months maximum
- Subject to a waiting/excess period (usually 30–90 days)

---

### 5.2 Buildings & Contents Insurance

**Buildings Insurance:**
- Covers structure of the building (walls, roof, floors, fixtures)
- Lenders REQUIRE this as a condition of mortgage
- Sum insured based on **rebuild cost** (not market value)
- Covers: fire, flood, storm, subsidence, escape of water, vandalism

**Contents Insurance:**
- Personal possessions inside the home
- Not required by lender but advisable
- "Bedroom rated" policies = cover based on number of bedrooms

---

## ─── UNIT 6: Mortgage Payment Methods & Post-Completion ───

### 6.1 Repayment Methods

| Method | How it works | Capital Remaining at End |
|--------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Repayment (Capital & Interest)** | Monthly payment covers interest + some capital; balance falls each month | £0 — fully repaid |
| **Interest-Only** | Monthly payment covers interest only; capital remains unchanged | Full original loan — must have separate repayment vehicle |

**Repayment Vehicles for Interest-Only:**
- Endowment policy (old; now largely sold off)
- ISA / stocks & shares portfolio
- Pension (take tax-free lump sum at retirement to repay)
- Sale of property
- Inheritance (not acceptable to lenders — too uncertain)

---

### 6.2 Interest Rate Types — KEY EXAM TOPIC

| Rate Type | Description | Risk to Borrower |
|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Standard Variable Rate (SVR)** | Lender's default rate; changes at lender's discretion | Can change at any time |
| **Fixed Rate** | Rate stays the same for a set period (e.g., 2, 3, 5 years) | Rate risk eliminated for the fixed period |
| **Tracker** | Follows Bank of England base rate ± a margin (e.g., BoE + 1.5%) | Rises and falls with base rate |
| **Discount** | Discount off lender's SVR for a set period | SVR can still rise, dragging up the discounted rate |
| **Capped** | Rate cannot exceed a set ceiling | Limited protection; no benefit if rates fall below cap unless "collar" not applied |
| **Offset** | Savings in linked account reduce the loan balance interest is charged on | Rate varies; requires discipline to keep savings separate |

**Early Repayment Charges (ERC):**
- Applied if borrower leaves a fixed/tracker deal before the tie-in period ends
- Usually % of outstanding loan (e.g., 3% in year 1, 2% in year 2, 1% in year 3)

---

### 6.3 Buy-to-Let (BTL) Mortgages

- **Minimum deposit** typically 25% (75% LTV max for most lenders)
- Assessed on **rental income** — usually must cover 125–145% of monthly interest at a stressed rate
- BTL lending = mainly not regulated by FCA (unless accidental landlord or lending to family)
- Consumer BTL = regulated (e.g., inherited a property and renting out)
- Stamp Duty: additional **5% surcharge** on BTL and second homes (increased from 3% on 31 Oct 2024)

---

### 6.4 Mortgage Arrears & Post-Completion

**Lender obligations when a borrower falls into arrears (FCA MCOB rules):**
1. Treat customers fairly; consider circumstances
2. Refer to free debt advice (Money Advice Service / StepChange)
3. Consider all options before repossession (forbearance)
4. Only repossess as last resort

**Order of events in repossession:**
1. Borrower misses payments → arrears accrue
2. Lender contacts, offers options (payment holiday, term extension, interest-only switch)
3. Default notice issued (need to be 3+ months in arrears typically)
4. Court possession order sought
5. Suspended possession order (SPO) — court can suspend if borrower makes arrangement
6. Warrant of possession → eviction
7. Property sold; shortfall is still a debt owed by borrower

## Case Study Technique — Step by Step

### The 4-Minute Pre-Question Protocol
Before reading a single question, profile the client:

```
Client Profile Checklist:
□ Names & ages
□ Employment status (employed / self-employed / retired)
□ Income (gross / net; salary + bonus; joint or single)
□ Monthly outgoings / existing debts
□ Property: value, location, freehold/leasehold
□ Desired loan amount & term
□ LTV (calculate immediately)
□ Any "red flags": adverse credit, vulnerability, unusual circumstances
□ Preferred rate type / flexibility needs
□ Protection in place (life cover, income protection, buildings)
```

### Time Management
- 18–20 minutes per case study
- Flag uncertain questions; return at end
- Never leave a question blank — no negative marking

### "Regulator-First" Thinking
> When two answers look plausible, always pick the one that:
> - **Reduces risk to the consumer**
> - **Aligns with FCA/Consumer Duty**
> - **Is the most suitable AND affordable long-term**

## Common Case Study Scenarios

### Scenario Type 1: First-Time Buyer
**Typical profile:** Young couple, one employed, one self-employed; limited deposit; want to buy a 2-bed flat (leasehold).

**Watch for:**
- Can they prove self-employed income (2+ years' accounts)?
- Is the lease length acceptable? (Most lenders require 70+ years remaining after mortgage term)
- Is leasehold service charge included in affordability?
- Should they consider Shared Ownership?
- What life cover is appropriate — decreasing term on a repayment mortgage

---

### Scenario Type 2: Remortgage
**Typical profile:** Homeowner coming off a fixed rate; wants to raise capital for home improvements.

**Watch for:**
- New LTV after raising capital
- ERC on existing mortgage?
- Could they port the mortgage instead? (Portable = take to new property)
- Is the capital raise figure correct? (New loan = existing balance + additional amount)
- Affordability still met at new loan amount?

---

### Scenario Type 3: Buy-to-Let
**Typical profile:** Professional wanting to buy a rental property; has existing residential mortgage.

**Watch for:**
- Stamp Duty 3% surcharge
- Rental stress test: rent × 12 ÷ 12 × 125% at 5.5% stress rate (figures vary)
- Consumer BTL vs regular BTL? (if renting to family — regulated)
- BTL not covered by FSCS/FOS if non-regulated

---

### Scenario Type 4: Equity Release
**Typical profile:** Retired homeowner, 65+; wants to supplement pension; no dependants.

**Watch for:**
- Equity release should ONLY be recommended after exploring all alternatives
- Solicitor must independently advise the client
- Interest rollup — can massively erode estate
- Ensure any benefits (Pension Credit, Council Tax reduction) not affected
- Lifetime mortgage vs home reversion — which is more appropriate?

---

### Scenario Type 5: Adverse Credit
**Typical profile:** Applicant has missed payments / CCJ / IVA.

**Watch for:**
- Standard lenders will decline — specialist/adverse credit lenders needed
- Higher rates expected
- Time elapsed since the adverse event matters (most lenders have rules)
- CCJ = County Court Judgement. Satisfied CCJ (paid off) is less severe than unsatisfied
- DMP = Debt Management Plan; IVA = Individual Voluntary Arrangement; Bankruptcy
