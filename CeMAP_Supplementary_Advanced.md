## 1A. Consumer Credit Act 1974 (Updated by CCA 2006)

### What it Regulates
- Covers credit agreements where the **borrower is an individual** (not a company)
- Includes: personal loans, credit cards, overdrafts, hire purchase, conditional sale
- Does **NOT** cover first charge residential mortgages (those are under FSMA/FCA mortgage rules)

### Key Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Regulated agreement** | Credit agreement covered by the CCA (under £25,000 used to be the cap — now REMOVED; most consumer credit is regulated regardless of amount) |
| **APR** | Annual Percentage Rate — the true annual cost of credit including all fees and charges; enables comparison between products |
| **Total Charge for Credit (TCC)** | All charges the borrower pays for the credit (interest + fees + insurance where required) |
| **Credit Reference Agency (CRA)** | Organisation holding credit history data (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) |

### Withdrawal & Cancellation Rights

| Right | When | Timeframe |
|-------|------|-----------|
| **Right of Withdrawal** | Unsecured credit; no reason needed | **14 days** from agreement |
| **Right to Cancel** | If off-premises (e.g., signed at home after face-to-face meeting elsewhere) | **14 days** |
| **Voluntary Termination** | For hire purchase/conditional sale — terminate once you've paid 50% of total amount payable | Any time after 50% threshold |

### Early Repayment on Consumer Credit
- Borrower can repay early at any time
- Lender can charge a maximum rebate reduction of **1% of early repaid amount** (or 0.5% if less than 1 year remaining)

## 1B. Financial Promotions Rules (FCA)

### What Is a Financial Promotion?
Any communication that invites or induces someone to engage in financial activity — includes ads, letters, emails, websites, social media posts.

### The Golden Rule
> A financial promotion must be **fair, clear and not misleading**

### FCA Approval Requirement
- Must be approved by an **FCA-authorised person** before communication to the public
- Exemptions: communications between regulated firms, professional investors

### Key Requirements for Mortgage Promotions:
- Must include a representative example if quoting an interest rate
- Must include a health warning on risk (e.g., "Your home may be repossessed if you do not keep up repayments")
- Cannot make misleading comparisons

## 1C. Senior Managers & Certification Regime (SM&CR)

### Three Components

| Tier | Who | How Approved |
|------|-----|-------------|
| **Senior Managers** | Most senior decision-makers (CEO, CFO, Compliance Officer, MLRO) | FCA approved individually — must have Statement of Responsibilities |
| **Certified Persons** | Anyone whose job could cause significant harm to the firm/customers (e.g., advisers, risk managers) | Firm certifies annually — NOT FCA approved individually |
| **Conduct Rules** | Apply to ALL staff (including receptionists) | Firm responsible for training and compliance |

### The 5 Conduct Rules (Individual Conduct Rules — apply to all staff):
1. **Act with integrity**
2. **Act with due skill, care and diligence**
3. **Be open and cooperative with the FCA**
4. **Pay due regard to the interests of customers** and treat them fairly
5. **Observe proper standards of market conduct**

### Additional Rules for Senior Managers:
6. Take reasonable steps to ensure business is controlled effectively
7. Take reasonable steps to ensure the business complies with relevant requirements
8. Disclose appropriately any information of which the FCA would reasonably expect notice
9. Delegating responsibilities to appropriate people and oversee them

### Regulatory References
- When an employee leaves, the new employer can request a reference
- Former employer **must** state if the person was ever subject to disciplinary action related to Conduct Rules or SM&CR
- Cannot give a misleading reference (duty of candour)

## 1E. Pensions — Detailed Types

| Pension Type | Description | Who Pays In |
|-------------|-------------|------------|
| **State Pension** | Government pension; based on National Insurance record; full new State Pension requires 35 qualifying years | Government (NI contributions) |
| **Defined Benefit (DB) / Final Salary** | Guaranteed income in retirement based on salary and years of service; employer bears investment risk | Employer + employee |
| **Defined Contribution (DC) / Money Purchase** | Pension pot built up from contributions; no guaranteed income; investment risk is on the member | Employee + employer (optional) |
| **SIPP (Self-Invested Personal Pension)** | DC pension with wide investment choice; can hold commercial property | Individual controls investment |
| **Stakeholder Pension** | Low-cost DC pension; max charge 1.5% pa for first 10 years; flexible contributions | Individual |
| **Auto-Enrolment Workplace Pension** | Employer must enrol eligible workers; minimum total contribution 8% (employer ≥3%) | Employer + employee |

**Key Pension Tax Points:**
- Tax relief on contributions at individual's marginal rate
- Growth and income within pension = tax-free
- **25% tax-free lump sum** on taking benefits (remaining 75% taxed as income)
- Annual Allowance: **£60,000** (2025/26) — if you exceed, tax charge on excess
- Lifetime Allowance **abolished** from April 2024
- Can access pension from age **55** currently; rising to **57 from 6 April 2028**

**Money Purchase Annual Allowance (MPAA):**
- Once you start drawing flexibly from DC pension, MPAA = **£10,000** (not the full £60,000)

## 2A. Legal Charges — First & Second Charge

| Concept | Explanation |
|---------|------------|
| **Legal Charge** | Formal security over a property created by a deed; gives lender right to repossess and sell |
| **Equitable Charge** | Less formal; arises without a deed (e.g., informal agreement); harder to enforce |
| **First Charge** | The primary mortgage; first in line to be repaid from sale proceeds |
| **Second Charge** | Additional secured loan against the same property; only paid AFTER first charge is repaid |

**Second Charge (Secured Loan) vs Remortgage:**
| | Second Charge | Remortgage |
|--|--------------|-----------|
| Existing mortgage | Stays in place | Replaced |
| ERC payable? | No | Possibly yes |
| Best when... | Borrower locked into a good rate with high ERC | ERC low or expired |
| FCA regulated? | Yes | Yes |

## 2B. Credit Scoring & Credit Reference Agencies

### Three Main CRAs in the UK
1. **Experian**
2. **Equifax**
3. **TransUnion (formerly Callcredit)**

Lenders may use one, two, or all three — scores differ between agencies.

### What Appears on a Credit Report:
- Electoral roll registration
- Current credit accounts (balances, limits, payment history)
- CCJs (County Court Judgements) — stay for **6 years**
- Insolvency/bankruptcy — stays for **6 years**
- Searches (hard searches affect score; soft searches don't)
- Previous addresses

### Types of Adverse Credit

| Issue | Impact | Time on File |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| **Missed payment** | Mild | 6 years |
| **Default** | Moderate — account closed by lender | 6 years from default date |
| **CCJ (County Court Judgement)** | Serious — court-ordered debt | 6 years |
| **Satisfied CCJ** | Less severe than unsatisfied; still visible | 6 years |
| **IVA (Individual Voluntary Arrangement)** | Very serious | 6 years from start |
| **Bankruptcy** | Most severe | 6 years from discharge |
| **Debt Management Plan (DMP)** | Informal; doesn't always appear but can | 6 years after settled |

**Specialist/Adverse Lenders:**
- Higher rates compensate for increased risk
- Time since default matters — recent adverse far worse than old
- Lenders have their own criteria

## 2C. Houses in Multiple Occupation (HMO)

**What is an HMO?**
- Property rented by 3+ people from 2+ separate households
- Each person has own bedroom but shares common areas (kitchen, bathroom)
- Examples: student houses, professional house shares

**Licensing:**
- **Mandatory HMO licence** required if: 5+ persons from 2+ households (the old 3-storey requirement was **removed** in October 2018 — now applies regardless of number of storeys)
- Many councils also have **additional licensing** schemes for smaller HMOs
- Licences typically last 5 years; granted by local council

**Mortgage Implications:**
- Standard BTL mortgage usually NOT suitable
- Need specialist **HMO mortgage** or **commercial mortgage**
- Higher yield but more complexity, more regulation, more management

## 2D. Self-Build Mortgages

**Different from standard mortgages because:**
- Funds released in **stages** (known as drawdown/stage payments) as build progresses
- Property doesn't exist as security at start
- Higher risk for lender → rates typically higher

**Stage Payment Points (typical):**
1. Land purchase / foundations
2. Wall plate level (walls up to roof height)
3. Wind and watertight (roof on, windows in)
4. First fix (plastering, electrics rough-in)
5. Second fix (final electrics, plumbing, kitchen, bathroom)
6. Completion

**Two Types:**
- **Arrears stage payment** — lender pays AFTER each stage completed (borrower needs own funds to bridge)
- **Advance stage payment** — lender pays BEFORE each stage (easier for borrower, less common)

## 2E. New Build Properties — Special Mortgage Rules

**Typical Lender Restrictions on New Builds:**
- **Maximum LTV often lower** — many lenders cap at 85% on new build flats; 90% on new build houses
- **Incentives**: Developer cashback, fixtures & fittings, paid stamp duty — lenders include value of incentives in affordability assessment; if incentive > 5% of purchase price, lender may reduce loan
- New builds often carry **NHBC Buildmark Warranty** (10 years) — most lenders require this or equivalent (e.g., LABC, Premier, Protek)
- **Mortgage offer validity** — some lenders offer longer validity (up to 6 months) for new builds as build can overrun

## 2F. Foreign Nationals & Expat Mortgages

**Residential Mortgages for Foreign Nationals:**
- EEA/EU nationals with indefinite leave to remain or pre-settled status — most standard lenders will consider
- Non-EEA with limited leave to remain — more restrictive; many specialist lenders
- Key question: what is their right to reside in the UK long term?

**Expat Mortgages (UK Citizens Living Abroad):**
- Want to buy/retain UK property
- Income often paid in foreign currency — exchange rate risk
- Need specialist lender or offshore lender
- Stamp duty rules same as UK resident if not buying another property

## 2G. Mortgage Portability & Overpayments (Full Detail)

### Portability
- **Portable mortgage** = you can take the existing mortgage deal to a new property
- NOT guaranteed — lender must agree based on new affordability assessment
- If new property more expensive: top up at current market rates
- If new property cheaper: may still pay ERC on the portion reduced

**Practical Example:**
> Current mortgage: £200,000 fixed at 2.5% | New property: £220,000 | Additional £20,000 at new rate (e.g., 5.5%)
> Result: **Blended rate** across two portions

### Overpayments
- Most mortgages allow overpayments of **10% of outstanding balance per year** without ERC
- Above this → ERC triggered
- **Offset mortgage**: savings in linked account reduce the balance interest is charged on (but savings earn no interest)
- Daily interest calculation is more beneficial than annual (most modern mortgages use daily)

## 2H. Mortgage Market Review (MMR) 2014 — Key Rules

The MMR overhauled mortgage regulation. Core changes:

1. **Affordability assessment mandatory** — income AND outgoings, not just income multiple
2. **Stress testing required** — borrower must afford payments if rate rises (typically +3%)
3. **Interest-only**: repayment vehicle must be credible and lender-verified
4. **Advised sales** became the norm — non-advised (execution-only) allowed only in limited circumstances:
   - Customer requests it explicitly
   - Product is not a high-risk mortgage
   - Adviser documents why non-advised was appropriate

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Tied adviser** | Can only recommend products from one provider |
| **Multi-tied adviser** | Can recommend from a panel of providers |
| **Independent / Whole of market** | Considers all regulated mortgage products on the market |

**Initial Disclosure Document (IDD) / Disclosure Requirements:**
At the first meeting, adviser must disclose:
- Whether they are independent or restricted
- How they are remunerated (fee, commission, or both)

## 2J. The Mortgage Offer & Its Components

A formal **mortgage offer** contains:
- Loan amount
- Property address
- Interest rate and type
- Term
- Monthly payment
- Any special conditions (e.g., retentions, building works required)
- **Validity period** — typically **6 months** for standard; up to 12 months sometimes for new builds

**Offer Conditions:**
- **Retention**: lender holds back part of the loan until specified repairs completed
- **Personal guarantee**: sometimes required for limited company BTL
- **Redemption statement**: required to calculate exact amount to repay existing mortgage

## 2M. FCA Mortgage Rule Review (MRR) 2025 — NEW TOPIC

The FCA is modernising mortgage regulation as part of its 2025–2030 strategy:

**Key Changes (PS25/11 — July 2025):**
- **Modified Affordability Assessment (MAA)** expanded to cover remortgaging with new lenders (not just product transfers)
- **Term reductions** no longer require a full affordability reassessment
- **Execution-only mortgages**: firms must now identify customers who may need advice but are pursuing execution-only, to prevent foreseeable harm
- Interactive dialogue no longer automatically triggers an advice requirement

**Future Proposals (DP25/2 — June 2025):**
- Exploring rent-based affordability testing
- Potential reforms to help first-time buyers and underserved groups
- Greater use of data and AI in mortgage lending
- Targeted reforms for later-life lending

## 2N. Anti-Greenwashing Rule (FCA) — NEW TOPIC

**Effective 31 May 2024:**
- All FCA-authorised firms must ensure **sustainability-related claims** about products/services are **fair, clear, and not misleading**
- Part of the broader Sustainability Disclosure Requirements (SDR) framework
- Four sustainability labels for investment products available since July 2024
- Not a core CeMAP topic but may appear in Module 1 regulatory questions

## 2O. Non-UK Resident SDLT Surcharge — NEW TOPIC

- Non-UK residents purchasing residential property pay an **additional 2% surcharge** on ALL SDLT bands
- This is on top of the standard rates AND any additional dwelling surcharge
- Example: Non-UK resident buying a BTL property pays standard rates + 5% additional dwelling + 2% non-resident surcharge

---

## 2P. Cash ISA Changes from April 2027 — NEW TOPIC

- Cash ISA limit for under-65s reduced from £20,000 to **£12,000** per year
- Overall ISA allowance remains £20,000 (remaining £8,000 can go into Stocks & Shares ISA etc.)
- For age 65+: Cash ISA limit remains £20,000
- Transfers from Stocks & Shares ISA to Cash ISA no longer permitted (from April 2027)
- Minimum age for adult Cash ISA now **18** (previously was 16)

## Case Study A — First-Time Buyers (Worked)

### The Scenario
> **Tom (28) and Sarah (26)** are getting married next year and want to buy their first home. Tom is a police officer earning £34,000 basic + £4,000 regular overtime. Sarah is a nurse, employed for 3 years, earning £28,000 basic. They have saved £22,000 as a deposit. They've found a 2-bed flat (leasehold, 112 years remaining) listed at £220,000. Sarah has 1 missed credit card payment from 2 years ago; Tom has a clean credit history. They want monthly repayments to be as low as possible.

---

### Step 1: Profile the Clients

| Data Point | Tom | Sarah |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Age | 28 | 26 |
| Employment | Employed, permanent | Employed, 3 years |
| Basic Income | £34,000 | £28,000 |
| Variable Income | £4,000 overtime (regular — most lenders include) | £0 |
| Total usable income | £38,000 | £28,000 |
| Joint income | **£66,000** | |
| Deposit | £22,000 | |
| Property value | £220,000 | |
| Loan required | **£198,000** | |
| LTV | 198,000 ÷ 220,000 × 100 = **90%** | |
| Adverse credit? | None | 1 missed payment, 2 years ago |

---

### Step 2: Key Calculations

**LTV = 90%** — above 85% but standard residential; most high-street lenders will consider but rate will be higher.

**Typical income multiple at 4.5x joint income:**
£66,000 × 4.5 = £297,000 — well above the needed £198,000 ✅

**Affordability stress test** (assume current rate 4.5% + 3% stress = 7.5%):
£198,000 at 7.5% over 25 years ≈ £1,455/month
Combined net monthly income approximately £4,000 — affordable ✅

**Sarah's adverse credit:**
- 1 missed payment 2 years ago — relatively minor
- Most mainstream lenders will accept if only 1 occurrence and settled
- Specialist lender not required

---

### Step 3: Key Advice Points

**Mortgage type:**
- Repayment mortgage recommended (builds equity; fully repaid at term end)
- Fixed rate — provides certainty as they start married life, budget predictability

**Term:**
- 25–35 year term — longer term = lower monthly payment (meets stated objective)
- BUT total interest cost higher over life of mortgage — adviser must make this clear

**Leasehold considerations:**
- 112 years remaining is fine for most lenders (requirement: usually 70+ years after mortgage term ends)
- 25-year mortgage: need 25 + 70 = 95 years remaining minimum → 112 years is fine ✅
- Service charge and ground rent must be checked and included in affordability

**Protection:**
- Both employed — life cover critical
- **Decreasing term assurance** on a repayment mortgage (cover reduces as loan reduces)
- Joint life, first death policy recommended for simplicity
- Income protection — nursing and policing both moderate risk; maintain income if sick
- Buildings insurance: lender will require this before completion

**Stamp Duty:**
- Purchase price £220,000
- FTBs: 0% up to £300,000 (FTB SDLT relief threshold from April 2025)
- **SDLT = £0** for this purchase as FTBs under the threshold ✅

---

## Case Study B — Remortgage with Capital Raising (Worked)

### The Scenario
> **James (42)** owns a home valued at £350,000. He has an outstanding mortgage balance of £130,000, fixed at 1.8% with 14 months remaining on the fix. His ERC is 1% of outstanding balance. He wants to borrow an additional £50,000 to build an extension. He earns £65,000 a year as a commercial manager (employed 8 years). Monthly outgoings: £800 existing mortgage, £300 car loan (18 months remaining), £150 credit card minimum payment.

---

### Step 1: Profile

| Data Point | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Income | £65,000 |
| Property Value | £350,000 |
| Existing balance | £130,000 |
| Additional borrowing | £50,000 |
| **New total loan** | **£180,000** |
| **New LTV** | 180,000 ÷ 350,000 × 100 = **51.4%** |
| Current rate | 1.8% fixed, 14 months remaining |
| ERC | 1% of £130,000 = **£1,300** |

---

### Step 2: Analysis

**Option A — Remortgage now:**
- ERC of £1,300 payable now
- New deal will be at current market rates (likely 4–5%+)
- Monthly payment increases significantly
- Only makes sense if capital urgently needed now

**Option B — Wait 14 months, then remortgage:**
- No ERC
- New deal still at market rate but no upfront cost
- Extension delayed 14 months

**Option C — Second Charge Loan:**
- Take equity loan of £50,000 secured on property (second charge)
- Existing first charge mortgage stays at 1.8% — ERC avoided
- Second charge loan at a higher rate (secured loans typically 7–12%)
- But existing cheap rate preserved on £130,000

**Best advice:** If extension is needed now, **second charge** is likely most cost-effective (preserves the 1.8% deal). If willing to wait 14 months, **remortgage at fix expiry** is cleanest solution.

---

### Exam-Style Questions

**Q1:** The ERC on James's existing mortgage is:
- A) £500 → B) **£1,300** ✅ → C) £2,340 → D) £130

**Q2:** The new LTV after raising the additional £50,000 will be:
- A) 37.1% → B) 45% → C) **51.4%** ✅ → D) 60%

**Q3:** To retain the existing 1.8% rate while still raising £50,000 now, James should consider:
- A) Remortgaging immediately and paying the ERC
- B) An unsecured personal loan
- C) Waiting 14 months and doing nothing
- D) **A second charge secured loan** ✅

**Q4:** James's car loan with 18 months remaining:
- A) Must be fully repaid before a mortgage is offered
- B) Cannot be considered in affordability assessment
- C) **Is included as a committed expenditure in the affordability assessment** ✅
- D) Automatically disqualifies him from borrowing more

## Case Study C — Equity Release (Worked)

### The Scenario
> **Margaret (71)** is a widow who owns her property outright, valued at £380,000. Her State Pension is £11,500/year and she has a small occupational pension of £4,200/year. She wants to raise £60,000 to help her daughter buy her first home and to have some savings for care costs. She is in reasonable health. She has no dependants who rely on her for income. Her daughter and son-in-law will receive the property in her will.

---

### Step 1: Profile

| Data Point | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Age | 71 |
| Property value | £380,000 |
| Mortgage outstanding | £0 |
| Equity | £380,000 |
| Income | £15,700/year |
| Amount sought | £60,000 |
| Family | Daughter (to receive property in will) |

---

### Step 2: Equity Release Suitability

**LTV available at 71 via Lifetime Mortgage:**
- Typical equity release lenders allow 20–50% LTV depending on age
- At 71: approximately 35–40% LTV = £133,000–£152,000 maximum available
- £60,000 is well within range ✅

**What happens to the debt?**
- Lifetime Mortgage: interest rolls up annually
- For illustration: £60,000 at 6% compound interest (closer to current rates)
  - After 10 years: £60,000 × (1.06)^10 = £107,451
  - After 15 years: £60,000 × (1.06)^15 = £143,768

**Inheritance concern:**
- Her children will receive a reduced inheritance
- **No Negative Equity Guarantee** (standard on SHIP/ERC member products) — estate never owes more than property value
- Consider a **protected equity** option: ringfence a percentage of property value (e.g., 30%) always passed to heirs

**Alternatives to explore FIRST:**
- Benefits check — is she receiving all entitlements? (Pension Credit, Attendance Allowance)  
- Downsizing — sell and buy smaller, releasing equity tax-free
- Unsecured lending from daughter (family arrangement)
- Grants for home improvements (if that were the purpose)

**Mandatory requirement:**
- Client MUST receive **independent legal advice** from a solicitor before equity release proceeds
- Adviser should confirm whether product is from an **ERC (Equity Release Council)** member

---

### Exam-Style Questions

**Q1:** The interest on Margaret's lifetime mortgage will:
- A) Be paid monthly
- B) **Roll up and be added to the loan, repaid when the property is sold** ✅
- C) Be a fixed amount each year
- D) Be waived if she lives beyond 80

**Q2:** Before recommending equity release, the adviser should first:
- A) Calculate the interest roll-up projection
- B) Check the property title
- C) **Explore all alternatives, including downsizing and benefits entitlement** ✅
- D) Contact HM Revenue & Customs

**Q3:** The No Negative Equity Guarantee means:
- A) The lender cannot increase the interest rate
- B) The property value will never fall
- C) **The borrower's estate will never owe more than the property's sale value** ✅
- D) The borrower retains full ownership

**Q4:** Which professional MUST independently advise Margaret before equity release proceeds?
- A) A financial planner
- B) An accountant
- C) An independent financial adviser
- D) **A solicitor** ✅

## 3A. Economic Cycle & Monetary/Fiscal Policy

### The Economic Cycle (Business Cycle)

```
Recovery → Expansion → Boom → Slowdown → Recession → Recovery
```

| Phase | Characteristics |
|-------|----------------|
| **Recovery** | Economy starts growing after recession; consumer confidence rises; unemployment starts falling |
| **Expansion** | Rising GDP, employment, spending and investment; prices begin to rise |
| **Boom** | Peak economic activity; high employment; inflationary pressures; asset price rises |
| **Slowdown** | Growth stalls; demand falls; businesses cut investment |
| **Recession** | Two consecutive quarters of negative GDP growth; rising unemployment; falling prices possible |

### Inflation & Deflation

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Inflation** | General rise in prices over time; measured by CPI (Consumer Prices Index); BoE target is **2%** |
| **Deflation** | General fall in prices — can be harmful as consumers delay purchases, businesses lose revenue |
| **Disinflation** | Inflation still positive but rate of increase is slowing down |
| **Stagflation** | High inflation combined with high unemployment and slow growth (worst case) |
| **RPI** | Retail Prices Index — older measure; includes mortgage interest; used for some contracts |
| **CPI** | Consumer Prices Index — official government/BoE measure; excludes housing costs |
| **CPIH** | CPI including owner-occupier housing costs |

### Monetary Policy (Bank of England)

- **MPC** (Monetary Policy Committee) sets base rate — meets **8 times per year**
- Raising interest rates → reduces borrowing → slows economy → reduces inflation
- Lowering interest rates → encourages borrowing → stimulates economy → may increase inflation
- **Quantitative Easing (QE)**: BoE creates new money electronically to buy government bonds (gilts), injecting money into the economy. Used when interest rates alone are insufficient. Increases money supply, lowers bond yields, encourages lending
- **Quantitative Tightening (QT)**: Reverse of QE — selling or not replacing bonds that mature to reduce money supply

### Fiscal Policy (Government)

- Government uses **taxation** and **public spending** to manage the economy
- **Expansionary fiscal policy** = lower taxes / higher spending → stimulates demand
- **Contractionary fiscal policy** = higher taxes / lower spending → reduces demand
- Set by **HM Treasury** and announced in the Budget and Autumn Statement

### Supply & Demand

- **Supply** = amount of goods/services available
- **Demand** = desire for goods/services backed by ability to pay
- When demand exceeds supply → prices rise (e.g., housing market)
- When supply exceeds demand → prices fall

## 3B. Legal Capacity & Vulnerable Customers

### Legal Capacity to Enter a Mortgage

| Person | Can they get a mortgage? |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Adult (18+), mentally capable** | Yes — full capacity |
| **Under 18 (minor)** | No — cannot hold a legal estate in land |
| **Mentally incapacitated individual** | Cannot enter contracts if unable to understand; may need Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) to act on behalf |
| **Bankrupt individual** | Severely restricted — most lenders will not lend; must disclose |
| **Company/corporation** | Yes — through directors; mortgage on commercial terms |

### Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA)

| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Property and Financial Affairs LPA** | Allows appointed person (attorney) to manage finances, including mortgage decisions |
| **Health and Welfare LPA** | Allows appointed person to make health/care decisions (not financial) |

- Must be registered with the **Office of the Public Guardian**
- Attorney must act in the person's **best interests**

### FCA Definition of Vulnerable Customers

> A vulnerable customer is someone who, due to their personal circumstances, is especially susceptible to harm — particularly when a firm is not acting with appropriate levels of care.

**The 4 Drivers of Vulnerability (FCA):**
1. **Health** — physical disability, mental health conditions, long-term illness
2. **Life events** — bereavement, job loss, relationship breakdown, new to country
3. **Resilience** — low financial resilience, low savings, unstable income
4. **Capability** — low literacy/numeracy, digital exclusion, limited English

**Adviser obligations:**
- Identify vulnerability indicators during fact-find
- Adapt communication style appropriately
- Ensure the customer genuinely understands the product
- Record vulnerability indicators and how they were addressed
- Consumer Duty requires firms to deliver good outcomes for ALL customers including vulnerable ones

## 3C. Property Defects, Restrictions & Building Issues

### Common Property Defects

| Defect | Description | Impact on Mortgage |
|--------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Subsidence** | Ground beneath property sinks, causing structural movement | Lender may decline or require specialist survey; insurance may be difficult/expensive |
| **Damp** | Rising damp, penetrating damp, or condensation affecting walls/floors | Lender may require remedial works before lending; retention on offer |
| **Japanese Knotweed** | Invasive plant that can damage foundations and structures | Lender decline likely if within 7m of property; requires treatment plan |
| **Asbestos** | Found in older properties (pre-2000); hazardous if disturbed | Lender may require survey/removal; may affect value |

| **Flat roof** | Less durable than pitched; may leak | Some lenders restrict LTV; require condition report |
| **Structural movement/cracks** | Can indicate subsidence, settlement, or heave | Lender requires engineer's report; may retain funds |
| **Woodworm/dry rot** | Timber infestation or decay | Remedial works required; retention common |

### Legal & Planning Restrictions

| Restriction | Description |
|------------|-------------|
| **Listed Building** | Building of special architectural/historic interest; graded I, II*, or II. Strict rules on alterations — Listed Building Consent required for any changes |
| **Conservation Area** | Area of special architectural/historic interest; extra planning controls on demolition, trees, and external alterations |

### Energy Performance Certificates (EPC)

- **Required by law** when selling or renting a property
- Rates energy efficiency from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient)

- Rental properties must have **minimum EPC rating of E** (landlords cannot let property rated F or G)
- Proposed future rules may raise minimum to **C** for new tenancies
- **Green mortgages** (see below) often require minimum EPC rating

## 3D. Debt Consolidation Mortgages — KEY EXAM TOPIC

### What Is Debt Consolidation via Mortgage?
Using a remortgage or further advance to pay off unsecured debts (credit cards, personal loans, car finance) by rolling them into the mortgage.

### Advantages
- Lower monthly payments (mortgage rate usually lower than credit card/loan rates)
- Single monthly payment instead of multiple
- Easier to manage finances

### Disadvantages & Risks — CRITICAL
- **Previously unsecured debt becomes SECURED on the home** — risk of repossession if payments missed
- **Total cost usually HIGHER** — because debt is spread over 20-30 years instead of 3-5 years
- May encourage re-borrowing (behavioural risk)
- Fees for remortgage/further advance (arrangement, valuation, legal)
- May trigger ERCs on existing mortgage

### Adviser Obligation
- Must clearly explain that **unsecured debts are becoming secured**
- Must calculate and show the **total cost comparison** (short-term loan vs mortgage term)
- Must assess whether client will accumulate debt again
- Must consider whether debt consolidation is **genuinely in the client's best interest**
- Must comply with Consumer Duty — deliver good outcomes
- If consolidating via second charge loan, MCOB debt consolidation rules require debts to be either repaid or included in affordability as if not repaid

### Suitability Letter Requirement
- Adviser **must** issue a suitability letter (also called suitability report)
- Must explain: why the recommended product is suitable, risks identified, alternatives considered
- Must be provided **before** the customer commits to the mortgage

## 3E. Suitability Letters & Record-Keeping

### What Is a Suitability Letter?
A written document provided to the customer after advising, explaining:
- The customer's needs, objectives, and circumstances
- Why the recommended mortgage product is suitable
- The risks considered
- Alternatives that were considered and why they were not recommended
- How affordability was assessed

### When Must It Be Provided?
- After regulated mortgage advice
- Before the customer is legally committed
- Copy must be retained for a minimum of **3 years** from conclusion of the regulated mortgage contract (MCOB record-keeping rules; some firms keep longer)

### Key Content
- Customer's name, property details, loan details
- Rate type and why chosen
- Term and why chosen
- Repayment method and why chosen
- Protection products recommended and why
- Risk warnings (especially for interest-only, debt consolidation, variable rates)

## 3F. Flexible Mortgages & Current Account Mortgages

### Flexible Mortgage Features

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Overpayments** | Make payments above the regular amount; reduces balance and interest faster |
| **Underpayments** | Pay less than regular amount for a period (usually requires overpayment reserve) |
| **Payment holidays** | Temporarily stop payments (usually after building up overpayment reserve; typically 1-6 months) |
| **Drawdown** | Borrow back money previously overpaid |
| **Daily interest calculation** | Interest calculated daily on remaining balance — more beneficial than annual/monthly |

**Advantages:** Suits irregular earners (self-employed, commission-based), allows early repayment without ERC
**Disadvantages:** Often higher interest rates than standard products; requires discipline

### Current Account Mortgage (CAM)

- Mortgage, savings, and current account all combined into one facility
- Salary paid in reduces the mortgage balance on which interest is charged
- As money is spent from current account, the balance rises again
- Similar concept to offset but fully integrated
- **Advantage:** Very effective interest reduction if salary is significant relative to mortgage
- **Disadvantage:** Complex; no separate savings (can feel like you have no money); requires financial discipline

### Offset Mortgage vs Current Account Mortgage

| | Offset | Current Account |
|--|--------|-----------------|
| **Savings account** | Separate but linked | Merged into one account |
| **Savings accessible?** | Yes | Yes — it's your current account |
| **Interest earned on savings?** | No | No |
| **Psychological effect** | Can still "see" savings | May feel like savings don't exist |
| **Tax benefit** | No interest earned = nothing to tax | Same |

## 3G. Bridging Finance

### What Is Bridging Finance?
Short-term secured loan used to "bridge" a gap — typically when buying a new property before selling the existing one.

### Key Features

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Term** | Usually 1-18 months |
| **Interest rates** | Higher than standard mortgages (typically 0.5-1.5% per MONTH) |
| **Security** | Secured against property (the one being sold, the one being bought, or both) |
| **Exit strategy** | Must have clear plan to repay (sale of property, remortgage, development sale) |
| **Regulation** | Regulated if borrower/family will occupy property; unregulated if investment/commercial |

### Types of Bridging Loan

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Open bridge** | No fixed repayment date (but usually max 12 months); higher rates |
| **Closed bridge** | Fixed repayment date (e.g., when property sale completes on a set date); lower rates |

### When Is Bridging Finance Used?
- Buying before selling (chain break)
- Property auction purchases (must complete within 28 days)
- Property renovation/development
- Short-term funding while awaiting a mortgage

### Risks
- Very expensive if not repaid quickly
- If property doesn't sell, borrower faces significant cost
- Monthly "rolled-up" interest can be substantial
- Lender can repossess if loan not repaid

## 3H. Endowment Mortgages — Historical Knowledge

### How Endowment Mortgages Worked
- **Interest-only mortgage** paired with an **endowment policy** (investment + life insurance)
- Borrower pays mortgage interest each month AND premium into endowment
- Endowment designed to grow over the mortgage term and repay the capital at maturity
- Included life cover — if borrower died, endowment paid out to repay mortgage

### The Endowment Shortfall Crisis
- Many endowment policies sold in 1980s/1990s were **projected to grow enough** to repay the mortgage
- Actual investment returns were **lower than projected**
- Result: millions of borrowers faced a **shortfall** at end of term — endowment didn't cover full mortgage
- Massive mis-selling scandal; FOS received thousands of complaints
- FCA/FSA took action against firms; compensation paid

### Why This Is Still Relevant
- Some borrowers still have active endowment policies approaching maturity
- Adviser must check if borrower has an endowment and whether it is on track
- If shortfall expected, adviser must discuss options: top-up savings, extend term, switch to repayment, etc.
- Surrendering an endowment may not be advisable if close to maturity or has good bonuses

### With-Profits Endowments
- Invested in a "with-profits fund"
- Annual and terminal bonuses added
- **Market Value Reduction (MVR)**: penalty applied if surrendering early
- Less volatile than direct equity investment but lower returns than hoped

## 3I. Product Transfers

### What Is a Product Transfer?
- Switching to a new mortgage deal **with your existing lender** (without remortgaging to a new lender)
- Does NOT require conveyancing, new valuation, or new legal fees (in most cases)
- Often faster and cheaper than a full remortgage

### When Is a Product Transfer Appropriate?
- Borrower's current deal (e.g., fixed rate) is ending and they would fall to SVR
- Borrower wants to stay with existing lender
- Borrower may not pass stricter affordability tests with a new lender
- Under the FCA's Mortgage Rule Review 2025, product transfers use a **Modified Affordability Assessment (MAA)** — now also extended to remortgages with new lenders

### Product Transfer vs Remortgage

| | Product Transfer | Remortgage |
|--|-----------------|------------|
| **Lender** | Same | New |
| **Legal fees** | Usually none | Yes |
| **Valuation** | Usually not required | Yes |
| **Time** | Quick (days/weeks) | Longer (4-8 weeks) |
| **Best when...** | Happy with lender; want simplicity | Better rates elsewhere; want additional borrowing |
| **Advice required?** | Depends — often execution-only | Usually advised |

## 3J. Green Mortgages & EPC Considerations

### What Is a Green Mortgage?
- Preferential interest rate or cashback offered for energy-efficient properties
- Typically requires **EPC rating of A or B** (some accept C)
- Incentivises buyers to purchase or improve energy efficiency

### Current Requirements
- All rental properties must currently hold **minimum EPC rating of E**

- EPC valid for **10 years**

- Advisers should check EPC rating during fact-find
- May affect property value and lender willingness

## 3K. Additional Case Study — Debt Consolidation (Worked)

### The Scenario
> **David (38)** is married with 2 children. He earns £42,000 as a warehouse manager. His wife Emma works part-time earning £15,000. They own a property valued at £280,000 with a mortgage of £160,000 (repayment, 2.9% fixed rate with 2 years remaining, ERC 2%). David has £22,000 of unsecured debt: £8,000 credit card (19.9% APR), £6,000 personal loan (9.5%, 3 years remaining), and £8,000 car finance (7.2%, 4 years remaining). Monthly debt repayments are £650. He wants to consolidate all debts into the mortgage to reduce monthly outgoings.

---

### Step 1: Profile

| Data Point | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Joint income | £57,000 |
| Property value | £280,000 |
| Existing mortgage | £160,000 |
| Unsecured debts | £22,000 |
| New total if consolidated | £182,000 |
| **Current LTV** | 160,000 ÷ 280,000 × 100 = **57.1%** |
| **New LTV** | 182,000 ÷ 280,000 × 100 = **65%** |
| Current ERC | 2% of £160,000 = **£3,200** |

---

### Step 2: Analysis

**Option A — Remortgage now to consolidate:**
- Loses 2.9% rate; ERC of £3,200
- New rate likely 4.5–5.5%; monthly payment increases
- Debts secured on home — **risk of repossession** if default
- Total interest on debt much higher when spread over 20+ years

**Option B — Wait 2 years, then remortgage:**
- No ERC; debts may be reduced by then
- But paying high credit card interest (19.9%) for 2 years

**Option C — Further advance from existing lender:**
- Keep existing rate on £160,000; new rate only on £22,000
- May avoid ERC if lender treats it as additional borrowing
- Still secures unsecured debt

**Option D — Do NOT consolidate:**
- Debts will be repaid in 3-4 years at current rates
- Total cost of debts shorter-term = lower than mortgage-term
- Monthly outgoings higher short-term but better long-term

### Step 3: Best Advice
- Calculate total cost: £22,000 at average 12% over 4 years vs. same at 5% over remaining mortgage term (say 18 years)
- The short-term approach (keep debts separate) likely costs LESS total interest
- **Consolidation is NOT automatically suitable** — adviser must prove it genuinely benefits the client
- If David's monthly cash flow is genuinely unsustainable, consolidation may be appropriate as a last resort
- Adviser MUST explain debts become secured and total cost likely increases

# 🃏 MODULE-SPECIFIC FLASHCARD SETS

```
Q: What are the 11 FCA Principles for Businesses?
A: 1. Integrity, 2. Skill/Care/Diligence, 3. Management & Control, 4. Financial Prudence, 5. Market Conduct, 6. Customers' Interests, 7. Communications, 8. Conflicts of Interest, 9. Customers: Relationships of Trust, 10. Clients' Assets, 11. Relations with Regulators. PLUS the new Principle 12: Consumer Duty

Q: What is the difference between the FCA and PRA?
A: FCA = conduct regulator (how firms treat customers). PRA = prudential regulator (financial soundness of banks/insurers). Both created by Financial Services Act 2012

Q: What is a regulated mortgage contract? (3 criteria)
A: 1. Lender provides credit to an individual, 2. Obligation secured on land in the UK, 3. At least 40% of the land is used as a dwelling

Q: What must a suitability letter contain?
A: Client's needs/objectives, why the recommended product is suitable, risks considered, alternatives rejected and why, how affordability was assessed

Q: What is the difference between a tracker and a discounted rate?
A: Tracker follows the Bank of England base rate at a set margin. Discounted rate follows the lender's SVR at a set discount. SVR can change independently of base rate

Q: What is negative equity?
A: When the outstanding mortgage balance exceeds the current value of the property

Q: What is the conveyancing process order?
A: Instruction of solicitors → Local searches → Survey/valuation → Mortgage offer → Exchange of contracts (legally binding) → Completion (keys handed over)

Q: What types of survey are available?
A: 1. Mortgage valuation (basic, for lender), 2. RICS Level 2 HomeBuyer Report (condition report), 3. RICS Level 3 Building Survey (full structural, most detailed)

Q: Name 3 acceptable repayment vehicles for interest-only mortgages
A: ISA, pension, endowment policy, investment portfolio, sale of another property, regular savings plan

Q: What is portability in mortgages?
A: The ability to transfer an existing mortgage product to a new property without paying ERCs, subject to the new property meeting the lender's criteria

Q: What MCOB section deals with responsible lending?
A: MCOB 11 — responsible lending and use of the interest rate stress test

Q: What is a second charge mortgage?
A: A loan secured against a property that already has a first charge mortgage. The second charge lender is paid after the first charge lender in the event of repossession

Q: What are the Pre-Action Protocol steps before repossession?
A: 1. Lender explores all alternatives (payment arrangements, term extension, interest-only switch), 2. Provides clear arrears information, 3. Allows reasonable time to pay, 4. Court action as last resort only

Q: What protection is most suitable for a repayment mortgage?
A: Decreasing term life assurance (cover decreases as the mortgage balance reduces)

Q: What protection is most suitable for an interest-only mortgage?
A: Level term life assurance (cover stays level as the debt remains constant)

Q: What is the key risk of debt consolidation via mortgage?
A: Unsecured debts (credit cards, loans) become SECURED against the home. If borrower defaults, they could lose their home

Q: What is the difference between a fixed rate and a capped rate?
A: Fixed = rate guaranteed not to change during fixed period. Capped = rate can fall but cannot rise above the cap. Capped rates are usually higher than equivalent fixed rates

Q: How does gazumping differ from gazundering?
A: Gazumping = seller accepts a higher offer from another buyer after already accepting yours. Gazundering = buyer reduces their offer just before completion
```
