# Japan in 60 Seconds — Treatment

Series: **History in 60 Seconds**, one YouTube Short per country. Style: Silkscreen Travel Poster.
Format: 1080×1920 vertical, 24 fps, ≤ 60 s, English narration (Kokoro, offline), subtitles printed as a band pull and exported as `.srt`.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One poster, many runs (chosen).** A single vertical print of Japan (stepped sky, sun, Mount Fuji, sea) stays fixed. Each era is a *palette re-ink* — a diagonal squeegee lays a new set of inks — and a new foreground landmark is pulled in the near ink, with the year printed into the info band. Fuji never moves; only the inks and the foreground change. Time passing = re-inking the same geometry, the style's native statement.
2. **A climb through loose planes.** One tall multi-plane print, camera craning from the rice paddies at the foot up the slope to a neon summit; each plane is an era. Strong move, but eras would be tiny slivers in 9:16 and the text would compete with parallax.
3. **Countdown of dates.** Eight big years printed one after another, each with a small vignette. Clear but list-like; no one image the viewer remembers.

**Why 1:** it gives the series a template (same geometry, new country = new landmark set and palettes), reads at phone size, and the one-image-many-inks idea *is* the history: the mountain is constant, the people change.

Opening: the empty screen — light through a blank mesh, the squeegee pulls the first sky band and the hook line lands at 0.4 s.
Ending: the **series wall** — all eight prints of the same mountain clipped side by side on a drying line, then a final card "WHICH COUNTRY NEXT?".

## 2. Logline and arc

*One mountain, eight printings: two thousand years of Japan re-inked era by era.*
Setup: rice, temples, samurai (warm earth inks). Turn: the black ships, empire and 1945 (inks drain to ash, silence). Ending: rebirth — the bullet train in bright inks, today's neon city, and the wall of every print.

## 3. Benchmark

- **WPA-era national-park screen prints** and **Fifty-Nine Parks**: learn — one template, palette per poster, restraint, paper as colour. Don't take — any composition, lettering, or park imagery.
- **Short-form history explainers on vertical video**: learn — a hook in the first second, one fact per beat, year always on screen. Don't take — stock footage, zoom-on-photo look, presenters.

## 4. Shot list (all inside one full-bleed vertical print)

| # | Shot | Move | Dur | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close on blank mesh, squeegee trucking down | truck with squeegee, slight roll | 0–3.5 | hook: colour arriving; title "JAPAN" printed into the sky |
| 2 | Full print, Fuji complete | fast pull-back as the screen lifts | 3.5–5 | reveal of the master image |
| 3–9 | Full print; era landmark pulled in near ink; info band prints year + era | locked, slow push on each era; diagonal re-ink sweep between eras | ~6 s each | layers stacking; one ink, one beat |
| 6 | 1945: inks drain to ash, sun turns to a dull red disc | push in toward the ruined dome, music drops out | 6 | silence before the turn |
| 7 | 1964: registration slip → snap as the train lands in register | snap + push to the info band | 6 | the comeback hit |
| 10 | Series wall: eight prints on a line | log-zoom pull-back + lateral dolly | ~6 | the ending; hold ≥ 3 s |

Transitions: diagonal re-ink sweep (blade, bead and a sliver of handle only). No dissolves, no cuts.

## 6. Cue map

96 bpm (0.625 s/beat), 4/4. Each era = 2 bars (~5 s) plus pickups to fit the line. Every re-ink sweep lands on a downbeat; each landmark pull lands on beat 1, the info band items on beats 2–3. Instruments by place: plucked shamisen-like strings and koto-like plucks (physical modelling), taiko-style drum hits, a breathy synthesised flute for the 1945 aftermath and the wall. 1945: everything drops out for a full bar before the line; first sound back is a single taiko hit on the 1964 snap.

## 7. Sound design

| Section | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | room tone, paper hiss | squeegee on mesh, screen clack/hinge | single pluck pickup |
| Ancient eras | room tone | squeegee per re-ink, ink squelch on landmark | plucked strings, light drum |
| 1853 | low sea wash J-cut in | squeegee, ship horn-like low tone | drum tension |
| 1945 | near silence, wind | slow dry squeegee | out for a bar, then breath flute |
| 1964 | — | registration snap (loud downbeat), rail swish | full: plucks + drums |
| Today / wall | faint city hum | drying-line clips | resolve on tonic, hold |

Music ducks under narration (voice ~10 dB above music by RMS). Master −14 LUFS.

## 8. Titles and subtitles

- Display: Big Shoulders Display 900 (condensed, OFL) for "JAPAN", the years and the wall card; Outfit 600 letter-spaced for era labels and subtitles.
- Subtitles are a printed strip in the info band, in an ink chosen by contrast, kept out of the Shorts UI zones (bottom ~360 px and right ~130 px stay clear of key text).
- Years in digits on screen; spelled out in the TTS text.

## As built

- Final length 59.8 s. Narration by Kokoro `bm_george` at 1.1×; every re-ink sweep is timed to land as its year is spoken (word timings from the speech check).
- Opening changed from the treatment: "JAPAN" is stamped first (hook at 0.1 s), then the info band, so the first subtitle is readable at once; the sky and the mountain print behind them.
- The score is in free sections, one per era, each filling its era exactly, so every sweep lands on a drum hit. 1945 has no music, only wind and a flute; the 1964 registration snap is the loudest hit.
- Ending: the eight prints shrink onto a drying line, and the card reads WHICH COUNTRY NEXT? / TELL US IN THE COMMENTS.
- Compared with the style's demo: structure, opening, camera path, score and ending all differ (it travels between cities; this re-inks one print through time).

## Revision 2 (YouTube review fixes)

- **Hook:** cold open on the finished 1964 print with the bullet train already sliding in on frame one and a drum hit; new first line "From rice farmers to bullet trains. This is Japan, in 60 seconds." The print is then re-inked back to the first era.
- **Shorts safe zones:** the title is smaller and lower (clear of the top bar); years and the end card are fitted left of the right-hand button column; subtitles stay above the channel-name/caption area.
- **Constant motion:** clouds and birds drift in every era, paddy water shimmers, Nara has falling blossom, the black ship sails and its smoke rises, the 1964 train arrives on the snap and accelerates away, city windows blink. No stretch of 2 s or more is frozen (it was 5.5 s before).
- **Subtitles:** 44 px → 60 px bold, at most two lines of ~28 characters, cut at phrase boundaries.
